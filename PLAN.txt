Tier 1: master deeply (core for both)

Ch 3–4 Sorting, binary search, sets, maps, heaps. Binary search on the answer especially.
Ch 6 Greedy. Huge on CF, common in hard interviews.
Ch 7 Dynamic programming. The single most important chapter for both. Go well beyond the book here.
Ch 8 Two pointers, nearest smaller elements (monotonic stack), sliding window. Interview favorites, and frequent on CF.
Ch 11–12 Graph basics, DFS and BFS.
Ch 14 Tree algorithms, including diameter and rerooting (14.3). Trees show up everywhere.

Tier 2: know well (separates good from very strong)

Ch 9 Range queries: prefix sums, BIT, segment tree. Essential for high CF rating, and a "wow" topic in tough interviews.
Ch 13 Dijkstra especially. Bellman–Ford and Floyd–Warshall just know.
Ch 15 Union-find (DSU) in particular. Kruskal comes with it.
Ch 16 Topological sort, DP on DAGs, cycle detection.
Ch 10 Bit manipulation and bitmask DP (10.5).
Ch 21–22.1 Primes, modular arithmetic, binomial coefficients. Mostly for CF; interviews rarely go deep here.
Ch 26.2 Trie. Common in interviews.

Tier 3: for high CF rating, mostly not interviews

Ch 18 Binary lifting, LCA, Euler tour on trees.
Ch 28.1 Lazy segment tree.
Ch 22.3 Inclusion-exclusion.
Ch 26.3–26.4 String hashing, Z-algorithm.
Ch 5.5 Meet in the middle.
